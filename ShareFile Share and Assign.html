ShareFile grew from files and folders into work-management containers — and every new surface invented its own way to give someone access. Share started doing assign's job. This is the story of pulling the two intents apart, and rebuilding people-and-access as one predictable model the whole platform can stand on.

MODULE 01 / THE PROBLEM & THE OPPORTUNITY
PROBLEM STATEMENT

The pace of building new features outran the thinking behind them. Assign and sharing workflow was left wide open — read as flexibility, experienced as confusion. The share modal became the assign modal, blurring two very different intents. Meanwhile sharing in files and folders — the most core thing ShareFile does — hadn't been revisited in years, still asking users to hand-build a permission matrix on every single share.

THE OPPORTUNITY — HMW

How might we design one consistent, intuitive way to share or assign anything in ShareFile — sharp enough to respect each feature's nuance, so users never have to relearn the interaction when they move from one part of the product to the next?

MODULE 04 / WHAT DATA AND INTERVIEWS TOLD US

We gave users a permission matrix(UI full of checkboxes) and full freedom. The data says they don't want either — they pick the same handful of combinations, over and over, and the flexibility we shipped mostly goes unused.

HOW ACCESS IS ACTUALLY GRANTED — LEGACY FOLDERS & PROJECTS
Create, modify & view — for everyone70%
View & download20%
Full access10%
Three combinations cover effectively everything. Custom permissions in the new features? A rounding error.
F-1

One permanent link does the job. We allow many; users configure a single one and decide whether sign-in is required.

F-2

Access gets overwritten by accident. Re-adding someone who already had access silently replaces what they had — usually because nobody could see what they had.

F-3

Access should cascade. Users expect what they granted on the parent to hold for the children — not to be re-declared at every assignment.

F-4

Assignment makes people nervous. Too many options, no sense of consequence — an anxious click on a screen that should feel routine.

MODULE 05 / COMPETITIVE RESEARCH

Share and assign is a platform primitive, so we deliberately looked past file-sharing products — into document workflows and work management — rather than inheriting the conventions of our own category.

GOOGLE WORKSPACE MICROSOFT 365 DROPBOX DOCUSIGN ACROBAT ASANA WRIKE
L-1

Two distinct paths, one experience. Share and assign are separated where it counts — the intent — and kept familiar everywhere else.

L-2

It starts with a people search. One field that quietly handles every kind of person and entity behind it — the user never picks a "type" first.

L-3

Link sharing is one click. Extra links and link controls still exist — they just move behind an obvious door instead of standing in the main flow.

L-4

Assignment reads its context. The container already knows the client, the team and the reviewers — good products spend that knowledge on suggestions.

L-5

Sharing is a subset of managing access. Not two screens — one continuous flow: everyone who has access, and the field to add more, in the same view.

L-6

Changes happen in place. Promote, demote or revoke without leaving the flow — visibility and control in one breath.

MODULE 06 / WHY THE BLUR HURTS WORK MANAGEMENT MOST

We assumed users would carry the distinction in their heads while we gave them a single interface for both. They didn't — and shouldn't have to. The cost shows up as an exhaustive option set on a screen where the user wanted to do one small thing, plus steps the product could have taken for them if it understood the intent.

WHEN I SHARE
Grant access

I want people to see and work inside a resource or a space. The audience changes with what's being shared — a folder is not a project.

WHEN I ASSIGN
Hand over the work

I want someone specific to do something specific — complete a request, fill a form, sign a document. Internal or external, it's a responsibility, not a door.

WHEN I MANAGE ACCESS
See and correct

I want the truth about who has access and what they can do — and the ability to change it right there, without fear.

THE PRIZE ONE PREDICTABLE MODEL FOR PEOPLE AND ACCESS — CONSISTENT, INTENTIONAL, THE SAME WHEREVER THE USER IS STANDING.

Building it from the model up.

MODULE 07 / THE CONCEPTUAL MODEL — SHARED WITH EVERY TEAM

Before a single screen, we wrote down what the two words mean in ShareFile — for every resource we have. This became the artefact other teams argue with, agree on, and design against.

MODEL · 01 TWO PRIMITIVES — NOT TWO NAMES FOR THE SAME ACTION
Share
Grants access.

Gives visibility and edit rights to a container or resource, so people can see and work within it. Nothing changes hands.

OBJECT OF THE VERB a place → the people who need in
Assign
Hands over responsibility.

Gives ownership of a specific task, item or request that somebody now has to complete. Someone is accountable at the end of it.

OBJECT OF THE VERB a piece of work → one person
WHY IT MATTERS Today, sharing silently assigns. Users can't tell which action they're taking — separating the two removes the ambiguity.
MODEL · 03 CONTAINERS ARE SHARED WHOLE — THE WORK INSIDE THEM IS STILL ASSIGNED ONE BY ONE

MODULE 08 / THE DIRECTION WE CONVERGED ON
D-1
One adaptive shell
SAME BONES · CONTEXT-AWARE SURFACE

The modal is one component everywhere. The title reads the context and the selection. The permissions change with where you are. And the tangential machinery — copy link, create link, link settings, multiple links — only appears where the use case genuinely calls for it.

SHARE "REFERENCE FILES" SHARE 2 FILES MANAGE ACCESS FOR "PROJECT" ASSIGN "ONBOARDING FORM"
FIG. 08-A · FOUR CONTEXTS, ONE SHELL — TITLE, PERMISSIONS AND LINK CONTROLS ADAPTHI-FI · DIRECTION
D-2
Presets, not a matrix
DATA-INFORMED · ACTION-ORIENTED LABELS

If 70% of shares are the same combination, the interface should offer that combination — not the parts to assemble it. Permissions collapse into a short list of presets named after what the person can actually do: Owner, Editor, Viewer for containers; Assignee, Reviewer for work. Each one carries a plain-language line describing its consequence, right where the choice is made.

FIG. 08-B · PRESETS WITH CONSEQUENCES SPELLED OUT — NO MATRIX TO ASSEMBLEHI-FI · DIRECTION
D-3
Assignment that reads the room
CONTEXT · GUARDRAILS · RECOVERY

A project is a smarter container than a folder — it knows its client entity and its team. We spend that knowledge instead of asking the user to re-supply it.

3.1

Suggest before you search. Linked client contacts and team members who could review surface as suggestions the moment the modal opens.

3.2

Make the impossible unavailable. A client contact can only ever be an assignee — so that's preselected and locked, rather than offered and then rejected.

3.3

Explain the edge, don't just block it. Mix an internal member with a client and the reviewer role stops applying — we say why, and how to get out of it.

3.4

Assigning outside the project is a choice, not an accident. Flag it, then offer both honest paths: add them to the project, or leave them with an isolated task view.

FIG. 08-C · SUGGESTIONS, LOCKED ROLES, AND THE OUTSIDE-THE-PROJECT CASE HANDLED IN PLACEHI-FI · DIRECTION
D-4
Everything else, on request
PROGRESSIVE DISCLOSURE

Messages, expiry, alerts, link settings — real needs, but not every time. They start as quiet chips and expand only when asked, so the default state of the modal is the 90% case: a name, a role, done.
